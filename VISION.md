# AEGIS vision

If we could snap our fingers and AEGIS were finished tomorrow, it would be a
self-hostable, personalizable Jarvis for anyone: one coherent intelligence for
a person, household, family, team, or other explicitly shared group.

A user installs Core, chooses models appropriate to their hardware and budget,
installs Packs for the parts of life they want AEGIS to understand, and uses
one persistent intelligence rather than a collection of apps and commands.
The user should not need to know Pack names, ActionCard IDs, or an artificial
command grammar.

The mature experience has natural continuous conversation with typo,
paraphrase, and informal-language tolerance; durable context and memory; goals,
projects, tasks, reminders, calendars, communications, finance, food,
groceries, pantry, recipes and meal planning; smart home and Home Assistant;
devices, infrastructure, homelab and vehicles; documents and knowledge;
CRM/relationships; travel; business operations; and explicitly enabled wellness
data. New Packs should arrive without Core redesign.

AEGIS reasons across these areas, composes useful workflows, offers proactive
suggestions and bounded background work, and presents the same world through
mobile, chat, desktop, web, voice, and ambient interfaces. The Constellation is
a visual representation of that world: a navigable central intelligence hub,
domain relationships, contextual resources, activity, and verification state—not
a second ontology or database.

Models are replaceable local or cloud cognition. Vaults and explicit shared
Spaces make privacy understandable. Actions are authorized, observed, and
independently verified; AEGIS never turns a plausible model sentence or HTTP
success into a claim that something happened.

The internals may be modular and deeply deterministic, but those seams should
support the human experience rather than dictate it. “Jarvis-like” describes
continuity, context, initiative, semantic understanding, and coherent
cross-domain assistance. It is a product analogy, not a claim of sentience.
